# What the live WiseQ website and public data actually say

**Observed 2026-10-29.** Everything in this document was read from the public
storefront or from Rob's own repository, on that date. Where a number could
not be read from a live source, it is marked as such rather than estimated.

This document exists because the delivery needs one place where the
**public facts**, the **repository facts** and the **transcript claims** can
be compared side by side. Conflating them is how a pitch ends up quoting a
figure that was never true.

## 3. Rules the storefront encodes as products

Three storefront "products" are **not sellable stock**. They are order
rules implemented as line items. Quoting them as goods is how a EUR 0 line
or a EUR 2.95 fee ends up on a school offer as if it were a product.

| Title | Type | SKU | Price | Tags |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Schoolbestelling | Schoolbestelling | `SCHOOL-TAMARISK` | 0.00 | intern, schoolbestelling |
| Administratie- en verzendkosten | Toeslag | (none) | 3.57 | b2b-toeslag, niet-los-bestelbaar |

**Correction to an earlier reading of this data.** The EUR 2.95 ex-VAT /
EUR 3.57 incl-VAT line was first read as a B2B surcharge. The live sample
page explains it precisely:

> *"De productprijs van een sample-koptelefoon is €7,05. Bij het afrekenen
> worden automatisch €2,95 verzendkosten toegevoegd, waardoor je op een vast
> totaalbedrag van €10 uitkomt."*
> — The sample headphone's product price is EUR 7.05. EUR 2.95 shipping
> costs are added automatically at checkout, bringing the total to a fixed
> EUR 10.

So EUR 2.95 is the **under-EUR-15 shipping contribution**, applied to the
B2B order because the shop adds a fixed administration-and-shipping line.
It is a checkout rule, not a per-unit B2B surcharge, and it is not a tariff
band. This delivery stores it in `order_rules`, not as a carrier tariff.

### Samples are a real B2B funnel — and one is priced inconsistently

| Title | SKU | Live price |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sample - Kids ANC Zwart | `Sample.Kids.ANC.Black` | 20.00 |
| Sample - Unbreakable - Blauw | `Sample.Unbreakable.Blue` | 10.00 |
| Sample - Hero - Blauw | `Sample.Hero.Blue` | 10.00 |
| Sample - Junior - Zwart | `Sample.Junior.Black` | 10.00 |
| Sample - One - Zwart-Blauw | `sample.one.zwart.blauw` | 10.00 |

The sample page states: one sample per customer, EUR 10 total (EUR 20 for
Kids ANC), free shipping above EUR 15, invoice payment, 14-day payment
terms, delivered in 1–2 working days, and **staffel discount from 10 units**.

**An inconsistency worth Rob's attention:** the page says the product price
is EUR 7.05 and EUR 2.95 shipping is added on top, but four of the five
sample products are priced at **EUR 10.00** in the catalogue. Either the
sample product price was changed to 10.00 and the EUR 2.95 line now makes
the order EUR 12.95, or the catalogue price is out of date. The storefront
answers itself: the FAQ says *"Waarom is de productprijs €7,05 en niet €10?"*
— so EUR 10 in the catalogue contradicts the published explanation.

**This is a live, customer-facing discrepancy on the B2B funnel.** It is
recorded here as an observation with a date, not as a conclusion about its
cause.

### The B2B mechanics, from the live pages

- Registration: `https://www.wiseq.nl/pages/zakelijke-klant`. The embedded
  `{SamitaWSRegistrationForm:...}` is **WiseQ's own Shopify app** ("Sami
  B2B Wholesale Pricing" by Samita), not a Dutch school-procurement
  registry. The staffelprijzen it serves live inside that app and are
  visible only after a buyer registers. See handover decision 13.
- Samples: `https://www.wiseq.nl/pages/sample-aanvragen`.
- Class sets: `https://www.wiseq.nl/pages/klassenset-koptelefoons-kopen-school`.
- Education overview: `https://www.wiseq.nl/pages/ict-voor-onderwijs-kinderkoptelefoons-tablets-laptopkarren-wiseq`.

The class-set page gives sizing guidance that is directly usable as a
quoting rule: devices + 2 for a shared room set; largest group + 10% for a
building; pupils + 10% for a per-pupil set; largest exam group + 3 for
digital exams. It also states to plan for **10–15% attrition per school
year**.

**The 10–15% is attrition, not a discount — and this is worth being exact
about, because it is the single easiest figure to misread.** The live
sentence is *"Reken op 10 tot 15% uitval per schooljaar bij intensief
gededeeld gebruik. Kabels knikken, oorkussens slijten, exemplaren
verdwijnen."* — plan for 10 to 15% loss per school year under intensive
shared use; cables kink, ear cushions wear out, units go missing.

An earlier internal note read this as a "15% school discount". It is not:
it is a *sizing* allowance, expressed against the group, not a price
reduction. The site's actual pricing mechanism is tiered staffel pricing,
visible only after B2B registration. No flat percentage discount appears
anywhere on the public site; the only other percentage in the storefront
is a **5% newsletter discount** on the homepage.

This matters operationally, not just editorially: a 15% "school discount"
applied to an order would be a number WiseQ does not publish and would
not honour. The implementation therefore ships **0%** with an explicit
placeholder flag, and the 10–15% is modelled as a class-set *sizing*
input (`src/wiseq/agents/class_set_sizing.py`) where it belongs.

Staffel prices are only visible after B2B registration — that is deliberate
and it is why the replica can only carry list prices.

**A detail the repository does not have:** the site sets a cookie
`wq_b2b` to switch the shop into "zakelijke weergave" (B2B view, prices
excluding VAT, B2B assortment). So the B2B pricing model is: ex-VAT prices
plus a staffel table revealed on registration.

### The key finding: the Scamadviser flag is circular, and the two sources contradict each other

This is the single most important thing in the reputation picture, and it
changes the conversation with Rob.

WebshopChecker lists exactly **one** external report on WiseQ. Reading the
page, that one report is **the Scamadviser flag itself** — dated 21-08-2026.
It is not a customer complaint, not a regulator action, and not a
chargeback. So the chain is:

```
WebshopChecker: "1 external report"  ──┐
                                       ├──>  same single Scamadviser flag
Scamadviser: "Very Likely Unsafe"     ──┘
```

**Neither checker is independent of the other.** A prospect who checks one
sees a scary verdict, checks the other to corroborate, and gets the same
flag back — reading as if two independent sources confirmed a problem.
There is one source, counting twice.

**What WebshopChecker actually says when you read past the headline:**

| Its own check | Result |
| --- | --- |
| AI risk score (dropshipper/scammer pattern match) | **0 / 100** — positive |
| Domain age | **7 years, 9 months** (registered 18-01-2019) — positive |
| Community reports | **none visible** |
| Geschillencommissie (dispute committee) | none found |
| Reviews in its own database | none — it says to check independent platforms |
| Keurmerk | not found in its sources |

Its summary sentence is the honest one: *"De totale waardering is slecht,
maar juist door de meldingen is het verstandig om extra onderzoek te doen
voordat je bestelt"* — the overall rating is poor, but because of the
report it is wise to investigate further before ordering. That is a
reasonable statement about **one** unverified flag, presented next to an
AI risk score of zero and a seven-year-old domain.

**Two concrete, fixable gaps** fall out of this:

1. **No keurmerk.** WebshopChecker looks for one and does not find it.
   WiseQ's own site carries a Trustpilot widget but no
   Webwinkelkeur/webshopkeurmerk badge. That is a cheap, high-value fix
   for a B2B seller: the badge is exactly what a Dutch school procurement
   checklist and a cautious buyer look for, and it is the signal the
   checkers were unable to find.
2. **The Scamadviser scan is stale** (3 months) and its own negatives are
   contradicted by the evidence — "no reviews found on commonly used
   review sites" is false (25 Trustpilot reviews, 163 bol.com reviews on
   one product), and "does not have many visitors" is false for a shop with
   230 live products and a bol.com store.

**These are observations, not diagnoses.** A Scamadviser verdict is a
third-party heuristic score; it does not establish why WiseQ scores the way
it does, and it is not evidence of any wrongdoing by the business. Its own
"popular products frequently sold by scammers" line is a generic heuristic
artifact — headphones and chargers are among the most commonly resold
products on earth, so any catalogue of them trips it.

**What is actionable, and what is not:**

| Signal | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| "Very Likely Unsafe" | **Actionable, and narrow.** It is one stale flag echoed by a second site. A fresh Scamadviser scan plus a keurmerk is the fix. |
| "No reviews were found on commonly used review sites" | **Contradicted by the evidence.** 25 Trustpilot reviews, 163 bol.com reviews on one product. The heuristic is reading a limited source set. |
| "does not have many visitors" | **Contradicted by the evidence.** 230 live products, a bol.com store, hundreds of bol.com product reviews. |
| Trustpilot 4.4/5 from 25 reviews | **Positive but a thin base.** 25 reviews means one bad cluster moves it a long way. |
| bol.com per-product ratings 4.5–4.7 across hundreds of reviews | **The strongest real signal.** This is the business's actual customer base, at volume, rating it well. |
| AI risk score 0/100, domain 7.5 years, no community reports | **The strongest counter-signal.** WebshopChecker's own engine does not see a scam pattern. |

**The honest read:** the reputation *risk* is not the customer experience —
bol.com and Trustpilot both show a well-liked shop, and WebshopChecker's
own AI engine gives a zero risk score — it is the **stale scoring artifact**
plus **no trust badge** plus a **Trustpilot count too small to be robust**.
That is a marketing and housekeeping problem, not a business-integrity one,
and it is fixable without changing anything about how the company trades.

**One retrieval note for honesty:** bol.com and Trustpilot bot-walled
anonymous requests (HTTP 403), so their figures above come from search
result snippets, from the subagent's cached page text, and from Wayback
captures of the bol.com store page — not from a live page read of bol.com
itself. The Scamadviser and WebshopChecker figures were read directly from
the pages. The audit script reports a blocked checker as *blocked*, never
as a zero score.

**Not verified here:** revenue, order volume, customer count as a measured
figure, bol.com seller rating at the store level on the live site, Amazon
listings, or any market-share statement. The claims ledger in the
repository governs those.

### What bol.com actually shows (dated archive, since the live site is bot-walled)

four CORS proxies — all 403/401). The only retrievable seller data is from

**Correction (2026-10-29).** An earlier revision of this document
presented the two captures as a time series of one account — "listings grew
~15% in six months (176 → 203)". **That was wrong, and it was my error.**
The two captures are **two different bol.com seller accounts**. They
share the trade name "WISEQ NL" and a correspondence address, but they
carry different KvK numbers, different VAT numbers, different start dates
and entirely separate review histories:

read two dated captures off two seller-page URLs that both contained the
string "wiseq-nl", saw the trade name match, and assumed a timeline. The
archive pages carry the KvK number, but only in a buried "Bedrijfsinformatie"
block that a line-oriented parse skipped. The fix is structural: the
parser now extracts the KvK, the seller ID and the VAT number from every
capture, the report states the identity split explicitly, and a test
(`test_bol_archive_never_renders_a_growth_series`) fails if timeline
wording such as "growth", "grew" or "increased" ever reappears in that
section.

| **School-ICT page** | **2 years** | **included** |

All three are live public positions on the same storefront today.

**Why this is not a cosmetic problem.** A class set is bought once and used
hard for years. If the school page is the operative promise, then a
headset chewed through by a child is a **supplier replacement, not a
warranty claim** — and an agent that routes it into a warranty flow
rejecting "fysieke schade" would be refusing a claim WiseQ has publicly
promised to honour. That is a school-trust problem, not a compliance
footnote.

**Two things follow:**

1. **Do not encode a warranty *term* without Rob deciding which is
   operative.** The system now encodes the repair-and-discount schedule
   that the internal manual actually specifies (see below), because that
   schedule is the business's real operating rule and it is the only
   source that distinguishes damage type, model family and age. It does
   **not** encode a customer-facing warranty *promise*, and it must not
   gain one until Rob settles which of the three public positions is
   operative — term length alone is not sufficient to route a claim, and
   "is physical damage covered" is still unanswered.
2. **Rob should align all three surfaces** to one policy. The school page
   is the one a school buyer reads, and the terms page is the one that
   binds.

**What the internal manual actually does (found 2026-10-29, and it
resolves the operational question).** `Handleiding_opzet.txt` carries a
full repair price list, not just a term length:

| Case | Policy |
| --- | --- |
| Broken, no physical break | within 2 years: replacement. Beyond 2 years: no warranty, 30% discount |
| Physical break, no specific cause — One/Junior/Hero | 0–6 mo: warranty replacement. 6–12 mo: 30% discount. Beyond 12 mo: 30% discount |
| Physical break, no specific cause — Unbreakable/ANC | 0–3 mo: warranty replacement. 3–6 mo: 50% discount. 6–12 mo: 30%. Beyond 12 mo: 30% |
| Break by the customer's own fault (customer admits it) — One/Junior/Hero | 0–6 mo: 50% discount. 6–12 mo: 30%. Beyond 12 mo: 30% |
| Break by the customer's own fault — Unbreakable/ANC | always 30% discount; raise to 50% if the customer does not agree |
| BT Wireless | replacement within 1 year; beyond that, outside warranty |

So the operative commercial policy **is** in the manual, and it is a
*repair-and-discount schedule*, not a simple warranty/no-warranty split.
It is now implemented in `src/wiseq/agents/warranty_policy.py` and
exposed at `POST /school/warranty-decision`, with every outcome marked
`needs_approval: true`.

**The gap that remains is now narrow and specific.** The manual's
"alles behalve fysieke breuken wordt vergoed" (2 years, excluding
physical breakage) is what the repair list implements. The school page
promises 2 years *including* physical damage. Under the manual, a
physically broken headset at 4 months is **not** a warranty case on
Unbreakable/ANC (it is 0–3 months on Unbreakable) — it is a 50%
discount case. But the school page tells a buyer it *is* covered. Rob
must decide which is operative; the code cannot decide that, and it
deliberately refuses to guess for an unrecognised model.

### WiseQ is mid-market on price, not premium

**The gap that matters is the school-grade channel.** Rkop sells wired
school headsets from **EUR 9,89** at volume tier, with a replaceable cable
as its durability strategy — same outcome as "super durable", at roughly
half WiseQ's price. A class set of 25 is ~EUR 250 at Rkop versus ~EUR 500
at WiseQ.

**So the differentiator cannot be unit price.** It is the service package:
staffel pricing, a free proefexemplaar, invoice terms, delivery to the
school address, and Dutch-language klassenset sizing guidance. That is a
services-led pitch against Rkop and School Supplier, not a product-led one
against BuddyPhones.

**And there is one clear, fixable weakness: warranty.** BuddyPhones gives
2 years (3 if registered); WiseQ's website says 1 year. But see the
warranty section above — WiseQ's own Amazon listings and Rob's internal
guide both say 2 years, so the website is the thing that is out of date,
and closing that gap is free.

### There is no 15% school discount on the live site

**This corrects an assumption that was in circulation.** The live WiseQ
pages describe **staffelprijzen** — volume tiers, whose actual prices
appear only after business registration. The only 10–15% figures on WiseQ
pages are the **attrition allowance** for shared headsets ("10 tot 15%
uitval per schooljaar"), which is a sizing input, not a discount. No flat
15% school discount was found on any WiseQ page, and it does not appear in
Rob's 2026-10-28 transcript either. Treat it as unverified until Rob
confirms it from an internal source.

### The procurement route that matters

| Platform | What it is | Why it matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Onderwijs Inkoop Groep / DAS** | A Dynamically Purchased Agreement: register once as a participating supplier, then many school boards call you off under pre-agreed terms without re-tendering | **Highest leverage.** Converts "one school emails you" into a repeatable channel. Contact: DAS-Service, 0172 578 909 |
| **Schoolinkoop.nl** | Collective EU-tender purchasing for schools, with a supplier "aanmelden" flow | Route to a multi-school framework agreement, but category-led and a longer cycle |
| **SIVON** | The ICT cooperative for ~450 school boards; runs supplier management, DPIA, verwerkersovereenkomst testing, normenkader-IBP | Compliance credibility, not a sales channel |
| **Edu-V** | The privacy mark for data exchange in education (Privacyconvenant moved into it in 2025) | Only relevant if the tablet/watch carries student data |
| **School Supplier, Rkop, Opzijnkoptelefoon** | Direct school webshops with quote form, bestelbon acceptance, proefpakket, staffel, invoice | **The operational template.** WiseQ already has all of these — a genuine asset |

**The threshold is confirmed, not assumed.** PIANOo (Expertisecentrum
Aanbesteden) publishes the 2026/2027 EU thresholds: **EUR 216,000 ex VAT**
for supplies and services to decentralised government — which is what a
school board is. Below that threshold the binding constraint is not EU
regulation but the school's **own inkoopbeleid**, which typically requires
a documented, quotable purchase.

**The most-missed item on WiseQ's public pages:** a visible
**verwerkersovereenkomst / DPA**. Schools ask for it as routine; the
zakelijke-klant page promises only "bestellen op factuur en toegang tot
passende prijzen". Even if a headset falls outside the strict scope of the
Privacyconvenant, having the document ready is what gets a supplier through
a school board's checklist.
